Undergraduate Research


Overview: Working with a team of 5 under my Thermodynamics Professor Dr. Matthew Hall, I have worked on two research projects. For our first project, we were tasked with determining the most efficient running conditions of the UT Engine Group's single-cylinder gasoline engine. Using a National Instruments LabView program, we modified the spark timing, dwell time, air-fuel ratio, fuel injection timing and duration, and the mean effective pressure. For our second and current project, we are analyzing the current breakdown event of spark plugs under pressurized conditions to determine degradation mitigation strategies.


Throughout the research process, we dealt with several issues including the spark plug firing on the wrong edge of the engine stroke, LabView connection failures, the flywheel falling out of callibration, and engine knocking. Equipped with a stroboscopic light, digital osciloscope, encoders, a manometer, and pressure transducer, we were able to troubleshoot these problems using root cause analysis methods. For our first experiment, we changed the duration of the fuel injection, ultimately varying the air-fuel ratio. In our second experiment, we changed the spark control timing using a sweep procedure. Ultimately, we presented our findings at FIRE's annual conference to a group of researchers and engineering professors.

Currently, I am engaged in a research project where we are analyzing the current breakdown event of spark plugs under pressurized conditions to determine degradation mitigation strategies. This project is aiding an ongoing research project led by Gabriel Brito under Dr. Matthew Hall.

Suction Valve Release Mechanism - GD 2026 Internship


Overview: I designed a release mechanism used after factory workers complete a fitment test which separates the suction valve from the valve body while preserving the suction valve and avoiding a stored-energy release hazard caused by the previous removing technique.


Problem: Removing the suction valve insert from the suction valve after performing a fitment test is a stored-energy release concern if done using the previous procedure which was to pry it off with a putty knife.

Goal: Develop a tool that can safely separate the suction valve from the valve body post-fitment test and preserve the suction valve.

Product: 6-Jaw carbon-steel lever release mechanism. Worker applies downward force using the arbor press to an aluminum puck which uniformly presses on the jaws, creating a moment which levers the suction valve off of the valve body and into the cavity inside the aluminum puck.

Takeaways: Ease of use is critical, as the workers will be using this mechanism for every fitment test several times a day. To improve this, I designed and 3D printed a jacket out of PETG that fits around the jaw, creating a flat surface to use a soft-blow mallet on to drive the jaw into the crevice easily. Additionally, I conducted a solids analysis of the jaws, determining the forces it will undergo, and plugged these into the FEA. Once I got the FEA's max displacement value, I conducted a deformation analysis to see if the displacement value from the FEA was within the elasticity region of the jaw.

06/11/2026


Go/No-Go Gauge - GD 2026 Internship


Overview: I designed a tolerance-checking go/no-go gauge which workers use to test whether parts are meeting spec throughout their shift. This project was assigned to me immediately after the molds were reworked, so I completed the design and sent it for manufacturing by the end of that same day.


Problem: Haliburton rejected 73 suction valve inserts produced by the injection molding machines at Plant 27. Molds for the single-cavity and 8-cavity Haitian machines were reworked to the low-end of the tolerance to solve the issue, but we needed a way for workers to quickly visually inspect these new parts before sending them to Haliburton as individually 3D-laser scanning each part would significantly backlog production.

Goal: Create an instrument for factory workers in plastics to visually inspect the +0.000, -0.005 tolerance on the 0.180" dimension of the Z1290-6678K part that fits onto Haliburton's fluid end valve bodies before shipping them.

Product: Designed and manufactured an Aluminum Go/No-Go Gauge that workers use to check the 0.180 dimension of the Z1290-6678K parts coming out of the injection molding machines. This gauge took what was initially a 5-minute long inspection process and reduced it to a 10-second dimension check, creating a 97% reduction in inspection time.

Takeaways: Ease of use, manufacturability, and working under a tight deadline. I designed the gauge to follow the same structure of a toothbrush, making it ergonomic and easy for workers to use. As for the deadline, the gauge had to be designed immediately as an audit was being conducted the next day and we needed to show there was an inspection method in place controlling this dimension.

Technical Skills Used: 3D CAD [Autodesk Inventor], Precision Machining, Prototyping


06/22/2026 - 07/24/2025


Mold Preparation Extension Tool - GD 2026 Internship


Overview: I designed a 2-in-1 standardized tool for factory workers to prepare the mold for composite compression molding while preventing future near-miss incidents.


Problem: Workers walking behind the press to brush the mold between cycles is a near-miss for damaging the complex pneumatic system.

Goal: Create an extension tool for the wire brushes used in the press shop that enables workers to clean the mold from the front and side without going behind the press while also being lightweight, durable, heat-resistant, and adjustable.

Product: Aluminum-based extension tool with 3 adjustable lengths to access the front and back of the mold cavities without interfering with the plumbing behind the press.

Takeaways: Weight reduction for this tool was critical since the workers will be using this tool every 10 minutes. For the standardized version of this tool, it will be made from PEEK, a thermoplastic with high heat-resistance and roughly half the density of aluminum. Additionally, the inner rod will be hollowed for further weight reduction.

Aluminum Ball in Cube


Overview: This project tested my ability to tolerance, CAM, effectively follow set-up procedures, and learn to CNC mill. My tools for this project included Fusion and the PNC Tormach 1100. I learned to export NC programs, use soft-jaws, and explore different smoothening operations to achieve the ball in cube.


One of my biggest takeaways from this project was the importance of recognizing tool-wear. I broke my first endmill while conducting a scallop operation for the ball. Clearing one of the pockets, the compressed air access got blocked by the part right as the endmill reached nearly maximum engagement, causing severe gunking and snapping the endmill. Learning from this, now I take the time to review the simulated operation for situations that create excessive load for the tool before exporting the NC programs and executing the operation.
